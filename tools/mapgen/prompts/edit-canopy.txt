This is a top-down battle map for a tabletop role-playing game, seen straight down from
directly above ({setting}). The flat dark-green shapes laid over it mark where trees stand: each
shape is the crown of one tree, at exactly that position and size. The smaller, lighter green
shapes along the forest's edge are {shrubs} shrubs, lower than the trees, with the sun on them. Paint them as the closed canopy of
an old summer forest seen straight down, in the same painting style, palette and sunlight (from
the upper left) as the rest of the map.

- One continuous canopy: crowns press against each other and merge; where two crowns meet there
  is a deep, dark shaded crevice, never an outline. No outlines anywhere.
- Where shapes overlap, the bigger crown stands higher and casts its shadow onto the lower one;
  through the few gaps you look down into near-black shade.
- Each crown is built of many small leaf clusters (each about the size of a hand-span on the
  map, much smaller than the crown) that catch the sun on their upper-left side and fall into
  shadow on the lower right. Big crowns are broad and irregular, never a pile of round balls.
- Colour: rich summer greens of one family ({species}); variety comes from light and shade,
  not from different hues.
- The crowns' outer edges are ragged and leafy and may reach a little beyond their disc; the
  canopy throws a soft dark shadow onto the ground to the lower right along its edge.
- No trunks, branches or ground visible inside the canopy.

Keep everything outside the green shapes exactly as it is: the river, its banks, the road, the
paths, the bridge, the meadow and the cottage. Do not add text, a grid, people or animals.
